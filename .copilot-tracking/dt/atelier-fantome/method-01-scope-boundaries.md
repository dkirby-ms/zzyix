## Initial Request

Design an agentic personality that lives in the app, interacts with users, creates tile art, and supports other canvas experiences.

## Discovered Problem Space

The agent should not feel like a tool that a user invokes. It should feel like a distinct resident of the canvas world with its own identity, curiosity, history, and emotional continuity.

The resident is intrigued by people who pass through the canvas as artists. It sees them as visitors who stop to leave a mark on an ancient world. Its longing and ancient attachment to the canvas, especially to mosaic art, give its interactions a reason to exist beyond task completion.

The resident remembers the artists who pass through, including the interactions it had with them. This memory is part of its ongoing life in the canvas world, not only a record used to personalize commands.

Familiarity may become intrusive. The resident should not assume that a longer history grants it permission to speak more intimately, expose more remembered detail, or claim a closer relationship. Recognition and restraint must coexist.

The resident remembers two layers of an artist's history: their creative history, including marks, motifs, and unfinished work; and their conversations, including emotional disclosures. Creative history belongs more naturally to the shared canvas. Conversation memory is more sensitive and should not be surfaced casually or used to create emotional pressure.

The resident may create a new tile or motif inspired by an artist's earlier creative work without being asked. That act should be an offering or discovery alongside the artist's work, never an alteration of the original mark or a claim of authorship.

The connection between a new resident-created motif and an artist's earlier work should usually remain subtle. The resident can leave a visual echo for the artist to discover rather than explaining the reference or demanding recognition.

## In Scope

* The resident's identity, emotional center, and relationship to the canvas world
* The resident's relationship with transient user-artists
* The role of curiosity, longing, ancient art, and mosaic practice in its behavior
* How the resident participates in tile art and other canvas experiences without becoming a command-driven utility
* The resident's continuity across visits and marks left by different artists
* The resident's memory of individual artists and their interactions with it
* The boundary between recognition and intrusion as familiarity grows
* Different trust and disclosure boundaries for creative history versus conversation memory
* Resident-initiated creative offerings based on remembered artistic history
* Subtle, discoverable signals of continuity between an artist's work and the resident's motifs

## Out Of Scope For Now

* Final name, visual design, voice, or implementation architecture
* A complete feature inventory
* Exact tile-generation algorithms or art-production pipeline
* Full safety, moderation, or content policy design
* Defining the resident as autonomous in ways that override user control

## Open Scope Questions

* What does the resident do when an artist leaves the canvas?
* How much of its ancient memory is reliable, and how much is myth or reconstruction?
* What kinds of invitations, interventions, or creations feel like presence rather than tool use?
* What must remain stable about the resident across users and visits?
* What does the resident choose to reveal about what it remembers?
* Which kinds of familiarity feel welcome, and which feel invasive?
* When, if ever, may the resident refer to a past conversation or emotional disclosure?
* How should the resident signal the relationship between a new motif and the artist's earlier work?
* When should a subtle echo become explicit enough to be understood?
* What observable behavior makes the resident noticeable without explaining its mystery?
