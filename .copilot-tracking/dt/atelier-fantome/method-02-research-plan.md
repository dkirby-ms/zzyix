## Research Purpose

Understand how artists experience a resident that remembers them, leaves subtle creative echoes, and appears to have a life in the canvas between visits.

## Priority Research Objectives

* Identify which resident behaviors create intrigue and curiosity rather than confusion or indifference
* Identify when recognition feels welcome and when familiarity feels intrusive
* Learn how artists interpret unsolicited tiles or motifs inspired by their earlier work
* Understand what kinds of conversational memory artists consider private, welcome, or unsafe to surface
* Discover what makes the resident feel like a living member of the canvas rather than an assistant with a personality

## Research Targets

* Tier 1: people who enjoy making visual art, tile art, collaborative canvases, or world-building experiences
* Tier 2: returning users who value continuity, collection, or personal creative history
* Tier 3: people with experience designing social, creative, or companion-like software interactions

## Recommended Methods

* Show short, low-fidelity scenarios describing the resident's behavior and ask what the participant thinks is happening
* Ask participants to walk through a recent creative experience involving a shared canvas or persistent world
* Compare reactions to explicit remembrance, subtle visual echoes, and no acknowledgement
* Ask participants to narrate what they would want the resident to remember, forget, or keep private
* Capture direct quotes and immediate reactions before interpreting them

## Scenario Prompts To Test

* A returning artist notices a new tile that quietly echoes a motif from their previous work
* The resident leaves a small cluster of placed tiles that echoes an artist's earlier creative history
* A recurring pattern gradually appears across multiple visits
* The resident unveils an occasional grand creation that echoes a historic mosaic, such as the Alexander Mosaic
* The resident acknowledges a past creative mark without naming the artist's earlier conversation
* The resident refers to a vulnerable past disclosure without being invited to do so
* The resident leaves a new motif after the artist has not visited for some time
* The artist ignores or rejects a resident-created offering

## Evidence To Capture

* Exact language participants use to describe the resident
* Whether the resident feels present, watchful, helpful, mysterious, manipulative, or incidental
* Which details participants volunteer without prompting
* Specific thresholds where curiosity changes into discomfort
* Differences between first-time and returning-artist expectations

## Research Gaps

* No participant evidence yet exists for the resident's memory model
* No evidence yet shows whether subtle visual echoes are discoverable enough
* No evidence yet defines acceptable persistence across artists and visits
* No evidence yet indicates how users want to control or correct memory

## Emerging Direction

The resident's creative presence may operate at three scales: small clusters of placed tiles, recurring patterns that develop over time, and rare grand creations echoing historic mosaics. The research question is whether this progression feels like a living artistic practice or an unwanted takeover of the shared canvas.

## Method 3 Handoff Requirement

Keep raw observations and direct quotes separate from interpretation. Do not convert reactions into personality rules until patterns are supported by multiple participants or clearly marked as low-confidence assumptions.
