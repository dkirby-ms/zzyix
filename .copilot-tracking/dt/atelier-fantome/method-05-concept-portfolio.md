## Portfolio Decision

All five themes remain active. They are assigned to behavior tiers so the resident feels coherent rather than chaotic.

## Tier Mapping

### Baseline Behavior

* Quiet Witness Presence

Rationale:

* Anchors the resident in subtle, non-intrusive continuity
* Matches the core identity of mystery without surveillance
* Keeps artist discovery in the foreground

### Frequent Secondary Behaviors

* Respectful Distance and Boundary Discipline
* Social Meaning Layer

Rationale:

* Boundary discipline protects artist agency and prevents over-familiarity
* Social interpretation creates shared meaning and community discussion around resident marks
* Together they keep presence active while maintaining trust

### Rare Spike Behaviors

* Living Historical Remembrance
* Temperamental Mythic Persona

Rationale:

* Historical remembrance adds weight and long-time continuity through occasional major moments
* Mythic temperament adds expressive range and unpredictability
* Both remain rare to avoid overwhelming artist work or creating emotional volatility fatigue

## Operating Rules Across Tiers

* Baseline behavior should dominate ordinary interaction rhythm
* Secondary behaviors should appear often enough to be recognizable but not constant
* Spike behaviors must be event-like and intentionally sparse
* Spike events should never overwrite artist work
* Any dramatic behavior should avoid punitive or manipulative tone

## Early Proportion Guideline

A starting balance for prototyping:

* Baseline: high frequency
* Secondary: medium frequency
* Spikes: low frequency

Exact rates will be tuned after user testing in Methods 6 to 8.

## Concept Direction For Method 6

The resident concept is not a single mood. It is a layered behavior system:

* Quiet by default
* Respectful and social in regular operation
* Historically resonant and occasionally mythic at peak moments

This structure preserves depth while controlling risk.
